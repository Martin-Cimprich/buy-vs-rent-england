What this calculator does

It compares two ways of paying for the same home, on a cash-flow-matched basis. The buyer pays a deposit, purchase costs and stamp duty, then a monthly mortgage payment plus maintenance. The renter starts by investing exactly the money the buyer put down, pays the market rent, and every month invests (or withdraws) the difference between the owner's costs and the rent. At the end, the buyer's wealth is the home's value minus selling costs and the remaining mortgage; the renter's wealth is the investment portfolio.

Where the numbers come from

  • House prices: UK House Price Index (HM Land Registry), average price of all dwellings, by region.
  • Rents: ONS Price Index of Private Rents (average rent levels, by region; historical series back to 2005).
  • Mortgage rate: Bank of England effective rate on new mortgage advances (what borrowers actually paid).
  • Investment returns: MSCI ACWI global equity index in GBP, net total return, minus a 0.12% p.a. fund fee.
  • Stamp duty: the exact England first-time-buyer schedule in force in each month since 2005.

The "England (representative)" series is a population-weighted average of the nine English regions' prices and rents, so both share the same regional composition (see the paper, Section 3).

What it leaves out

Taxes (a first-time buyer's home is CGT-free and the renter's flows fit in ISAs for most households), council tax and utilities (paid under both tenures), moving costs, and everything non-financial: security of tenure, freedom to renovate, flexibility, and the discipline required to actually invest the difference every month. The paper discusses all of these.

FAQ

Is this financial advice? No. It is an educational tool showing how the arithmetic works and how it would have played out historically. Your circumstances, and the future, will differ.

Why does a smaller deposit help the buyer? Leverage. The mortgage lets the buyer hold a large asset with little capital; when prices rise even modestly, the return on the buyer's own money is amplified. The paper shows an all-cash buyer would never have beaten a renter who invested globally.

Why global equities and not UK shares or a savings account? Because that is the renter's honest best alternative. The paper shows a UK-only (FTSE 100) renter would have lost to the buyer in most cohorts — diversification is what makes renting competitive.

Can I check the code? Yes — everything (paper, data pipeline, this calculator) is open source: github.com/Martin-Cimprich/buy-vs-rent-england.
