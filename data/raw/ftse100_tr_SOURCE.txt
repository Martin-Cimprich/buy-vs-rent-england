WHAT IT IS (exact definition, for the whitepaper):
Morningstar "growth of 10,000" time series for the iShares Core FTSE 100 UCITS ETF,
GBP Distributing share class (ticker ISF, ISIN IE0005042456), in GBP, monthly
frequency, NAV-based TOTAL RETURN: all cash distributions (dividends) are treated as
reinvested at NAV on the ex-date. Base = 10,000.00 at 2004-11-30. This is therefore
an ETF-based total-return PROXY for the FTSE 100 Total Return Index (TUKXG), NET of
the fund's ongoing charges: TER was 0.40% p.a. until June 2014, 0.07% p.a. since.
It is NOT the index itself. UK dividends carry no withholding tax for this fund, so
net-of-fee fund TR sits between the FTSE 100 gross TR and any net-TR variant.

COVERAGE & VERIFICATION (run 2026-07-09 with project venv pandas):
- 260 month-end observations, 2004-11-30 .. 2026-06-30. Zero missing months, zero
  duplicates; every date is a true calendar month-end. First monthly return: Dec 2004.
  June 2026 is the last COMPLETE month (retrieval date 2026-07-09).
- Monthly returns: mean +0.649%; min -13.36% (Mar 2020, matches FTSE 100 TR COVID
  crash); max +12.65% (Nov 2020, matches vaccine-rally month). CAGR 7.20% p.a.
- Cross-check vs the ACTUAL FTSE 100 Total Return Index (TUKXG, investing.com,
  Aug 2010 - Jun 2026, 190 common monthly returns): correlation 0.9999, mean abs
  monthly deviation 0.028%, max 0.146%. Annualized tracking difference of the ETF
  vs TUKXG over that window: -0.31% p.a. (fees + NAV/reinvestment timing).
- Cross-check vs Yahoo Finance ISF.L monthly ADJUSTED close (market price, Feb 2009 -
  Jun 2026, 209 common months): correlation 0.9876, mean abs monthly deviation 0.42%
  (market price premium/discount noise vs NAV).

WHY NOT THE INDEX ITSELF: no free source with pre-2010 TUKXG history was accessible
(Yahoo has no TUKXG; investing.com TUKXG starts Aug 2010; FT's TRIUKX:FSI starts
May 2012; stooq CSV download and WSJ/MarketWatch are bot-blocked; Yahoo ISF.L history
starts Jan 2009). For the whitepaper use case (a renter actually investing in a UK
large-cap tracker) the net-of-fee investable ETF series is arguably the more
appropriate object anyway.
